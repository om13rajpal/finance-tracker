I · The board sorts itself

The hero's board does not end at the hero. Here the same grid reorganises: scattered characters with colour landing at random resolve, sweeping left to right, into four clean blocks. Then it scatters and does it again.

This is the only option that is the product's actual mechanic rendered as a picture, and the only one that makes the hero feel like the first act of something rather than a nice screen you scrolled past. On the real page the sweep is driven by your scroll, so you are the one doing the sorting.

The cost: it repeats the twelve-row sort section further down, which also shows things being filed — you would want one of the two, not both. It is also abstract: four colour blocks are not four named buckets, so the key at the foot is load-bearing rather than decorative.

II · The month as one bar

No scroll-jack at all. The four buckets are one horizontal bar, segment widths proportional to what actually leaves each month, and you can see all four at once — so the question "where am I and how many are left" never arises, because it was the wrong question.

It is the only version that teaches something. Fixed costs being visibly three times savings is an argument no headline makes as well, and it is drawn from the same numbers the rooms were already printing.

The cost: it is the least spectacular of the three and it gives back the full-viewport colour the page currently gets from this section. After a dark, dense hero it may read as a step down in ambition rather than a change of gear.

III · Four rooms, furnished

Keeps the horizontal travel and fixes what was actually wrong with it: the rooms were empty. Three small objects floating in an enormous colour field reads as a slide, not a room. So each one now carries the real transactions that landed in that bucket — a colour block on the left holding the name, a cream ledger on the right holding the evidence.

The next room's colour shows at the right edge, so the travel stops being a surprise, and the four-segment rule at the lower left says where you are without a counter pill.

The cost: it is the conservative option — the same pattern, done properly. It also halves the colour: a bucket no longer owns a full viewport, which was the boldest thing this section did.

My pick

I · The board sorts itself — because it is the only one that makes the page feel authored rather than assembled. The hero opens on a board of unsorted noise; this resolves that same board into four blocks. That is one idea told twice, which is what a landing page is supposed to do, and it is the thing no competitor can copy without also building the parser.

If I am honest about the risk: it and the twelve-row sort section are the same argument, so picking I probably means cutting or reworking that section. II is the one that teaches, and it is the one I would pick if you want the page to be persuasive rather than impressive. III is the safe repair.
