II · The board

A departure board for money. The whole viewport is a grid of mono cells flickering through real merchants and real amounts, and the wordmark sits on top of it in a pool of shadow. The one figure that matters is set in guilt-free pink and its digits tick.

This is the only concept that leads with the number rather than the mechanism — and the number is the reason anyone would use this. It reads institutional and precise: a terminal, a ledger, an arrivals hall. Money, not an app.

The cost: mono type used as atmosphere is a well-worn "technical" costume, and the grid is decorative — those characters are not really computing anything. It is also the most legible-at-a-glance and the least surprising of the three. If you want the page to feel expensive and serious rather than clever, this is it.

III · Four doors

No ink ground, no cream ground — the viewport is the taxonomy. Four colour panels, edge to edge, floor to ceiling, and the wordmark laid across all four in ink so the letters change background mid-stroke. The panels breathe, widening and narrowing against each other like a month actually moving.

It is the most extreme thing the palette permits, and it is legal: ink on all four measures 4.61:1 or better, which the taxonomy round already proved. No competitor in Indian personal finance looks remotely like this.

The cost: it spends the entire colour system in the first viewport, so the four rooms further down have nothing left to reveal — that section would have to change or go. It is also the least explanatory: a first-time visitor sees four colours before they know what a bucket is. High confidence, low hand-holding.
