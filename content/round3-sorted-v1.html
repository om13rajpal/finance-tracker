§ 01

Login · stage one

There is no password field, because the product has no passwords. Stage one takes an email and sends a six-digit code. The hero is the chip constellation: six categories in an off-centre arrangement around a ₹, drawn in 4px ink outlines with flat fills. It does not rotate and it never loops — chips fly in once, snap into place, then the dotted connectors draw. A spinner loops; a filing system lands. The SVG is static by default; GSAP only moves it, so with JS off or reduced-motion on, this is exactly what renders.

§ 02

The stage change, and everything that can go wrong

The email→code handoff is the only real motion opportunity on this screen, so it gets the whole budget: the headline and form leave to the left over 150ms, the code stage arrives from the right over 240ms on --ease-out, and the six OTP boxes stagger in at 45ms. Direction carries meaning: Change email reverses it exactly. Under reduced motion it is a 120ms crossfade with no movement. Errors are not raw red text — they are an ink-bordered notice that names the fix, and the field takes a 2.5px alert ring.

§ 03

Dashboard · the four real modules

Exactly what /dashboard returns — net worth, guilt-free money, budget vs spend, upcoming 30 days. No recent-transactions feed, because there isn't one. Guilt-Free Money is the emotional centre, not net worth: it is the only number on the page that answers a question you actually ask, so it gets the 108px figure and net worth gets a calm companion panel. Budget vs Spend is where the chip system earns its keep — one row per category, one chip per row, the bar filled in that category's colour. One rule keeps it from becoming a birthday card: chips colour categories; ink colours totals.

One honest constraint, made into a rule

A recurring item carries name · amount · nextDueDate · type and no category. So the chip in the Upcoming rail cannot be a category chip. It encodes direction instead — hollow with a down arrow for an expense, invest-green with an up arrow for income. Chips never guess. If the data doesn't know the category, the chip stays hollow rather than inventing a colour.

§ 05

The taxonomy, resolved

The Round 2 palette shipped six chips with two collisions: bills #F1A007 vs shopping #FBA130 (near-identical mustard/orange) and transport #5483D3 vs health #14199C (two blues). I kept all six categories — they are all real — and re-spaced the two offenders around the hue wheel instead of dropping any. The rule: any two chips must differ by ≥45° of hue or ≥25 points of lightness. All fifteen pairs now pass. And regardless of colour, every chip carries a distinct icon — under deuteranopia the amber, green and red chips converge, so the icon is the primary carrier and colour is reinforcement, never the other way round.

§ 06

The tether

Optional · not in the design above

Sorted's most unusual feature is that it reads your bank alert emails and files transactions on its own — and right now nothing on the dashboard says so. This is C's own answer, in C's own vocabulary. It is not the Ledger& rubber stamp and it does not import anything from Direction D.

The idea: the dotted connector from the login hero becomes the app's mark for "a connection did this, not you." Same 1.6px ink stroke, same 4 6 dash. A chip with a short dotted tether on its left means the row filed itself. One meaning, one mark, reused — the constellation you saw at the door turns out to be the machinery.

Dashboard header · with the tether ribbon

Three chips, three tethers, one sentence. The strip only appears on days the parser actually ran, so it can never become permanent furniture. On a quiet day the header is exactly the header in §03.
