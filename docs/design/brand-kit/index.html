§ 00

What Sorted is for

Most money apps tell you what you spent. Sorted tells you what you can still spend.

One person owns this. Nobody is being sold anything, nobody is being onboarded, nobody is being scored. The screen opens on Guilt-Free Money — what is genuinely left after the bills that haven't landed yet and the SIPs that go out on the 5th — because that is the only number that changes what you do next. Net worth sits beside it, quieter, as the number you admire rather than act on.

Sorted will
  • Put one number where your eye lands first.
  • Read your bank alerts and file them, then show its working.
  • Give every category one colour and one icon, forever.
  • Stay legible on the ninety-third consecutive morning.
Sorted will not
  • Congratulate you, gamify you, or send a streak.
  • Put an illustration anywhere near a live rupee figure.
  • Use a drop shadow to imply importance.
  • Animate a number into place. A figure that overshoots has lied for 80ms.
§ 01

Logotype & name

There is no logo. There is a wordmark and a mark, and the mark is just the product's own vocabulary at display weight — two overlapping chips, ink-outlined. Nothing is drawn that isn't already in the interface.

Full lockup
Sorted

Mark + wordmark, baseline-aligned. Chip overlap is −9px at 26px, i.e. 35% of diameter.

Mono / one-colour
Sorted

For favicons, email signatures, anything that can't guarantee colour fidelity. The chips go hollow, never grey.

Inverse
Sorted

On ink, the chip borders flip to cream and the fills stay. Chip fills are never inverted — the taxonomy is absolute.

Clear space & minimum size

Clear space on all four sides equals one chip diameter at whatever size the lockup is set. At 26px chips that is 26px of air. Nothing crosses it — not a rule, not a nav item, not the page edge.

Minimum sizes: full lockup 96px wide; mark alone 22px. Below 22px the 1.5px chip border stops resolving on a 1x display and the mark turns into two grey dots. Under 22px, use the wordmark alone.

The name is not confirmed yet

Sorted arrived bundled with direction C. Direction C was picked; the name never got a separate yes. This page is where it lands.

On record for it: plain-confident English with one syllable of warmth, no fake Sanskrit, no misspelled vowel. It describes the mechanic literally — every rupee is sorted into a coloured chip — and it describes the intended end state of a session. Sorted.

Against it: it's a common English word, so a .com is unlikely and search is crowded. For a private single-user tool neither of those costs anything.

Why the icon is not decoration

food (H334, L83) and health (H343, L50) are 9° apart in hue. They separate on lightness alone. That was reviewed and accepted — pale pink against deep raspberry is a real distinction at 30px on cream. But it means hue is doing no work for that pair, and under deuteranopia the amber, green and raspberry chips converge into three similar mid-tones.

So the rule is absolute: every chip carries its own glyph, always, at every size, including 22px. A chip with no icon is a bug, not a variant. Colour is never the sole carrier of meaning.

§ 06

Components

Everything below is live — hover it, press it, tab to it. Every example is drawn from the login or the dashboard; nothing here previews a screen that hasn't been designed.

Shadow policy

There are no shadows in Sorted. No blur, no spread, no elevation, no ambient occlusion, nothing that fakes light. Depth comes from strokes. The one exception is the stamp on the primary pill: box-shadow: 0 2px 0 var(--ink) — zero blur, zero spread, hard ink offset. It is not a lift, it is a stamp: the button sits on the paper and can be pressed into it. This exception is not a licence for shadows generally. If you find yourself adding a second one, you have found a boundary that wants a border.

§ 07

The tether

Sorted's most unusual capability — it reads your bank alert emails and files the transactions itself — is invisible by default. The tether makes it visible with one mark, used once, meaning one thing.

Sunday, 30 August

Two days left in August.

Filed themselves 3 transactions from your HDFC and Amex alerts this morning. Review

The strip only renders on days the parser actually ran, so it can never become furniture. On a manual day it is absent, not empty.

Where it comes from

The tether is the login constellation's connector stroke, extracted: 1.6px ink, dasharray 4 6, round caps. Identical values, no variation. That identity is the whole idea — the connector you meet at the door turns out to be the machinery. Do not restyle one without the other.

Provenance for the record: the tether was invented inside Sorted's own chip vocabulary as this direction's answer to the signature-moment problem. It is not an import of direction D's rubber stamp. 

/* the only three values that matter */
stroke: var(--ink);
stroke-width: 1.6;      /* --stroke-tether */
stroke-dasharray: 4 6;  /* --dash-tether  */
stroke-linecap: round;
i

A tether means exactly one thing.
“This row was created by the Gmail parser, not by you.” It is never a status, never a sync indicator, never decoration on a heading. One meaning, one mark.

Accessibility: the tether is decorative in the accessibility tree (alt=""). The meaning it carries is duplicated in the row's own sub-label — “filed automatically” — so a screen reader loses nothing.

§ 08

Motion

Three curves, seven durations, and one rule that came out of a real bug. Sorted has no springs, no bounce, no overshoot. The playfulness in this product lives in the colour taxonomy, not in the timing function — a financial figure that overshoots its final value has lied to the owner for eighty milliseconds.

Turn this on, then press the buttons below. Every demo on this page scales with it. This is how the press bug was found.
The stamp press · locked rule

translateY exactly the shadow offset. Both properties, one curve. Never scale.

Any component carrying box-shadow:0 2px 0 var(--ink) presses by translating down exactly 2px while the shadow collapses to 0 0 0. Three things must be true at once, and each one was a separate bug before it was a rule.

Correct · ships

translateY only, both properties transitioned. translate and shadow-offset are complementary: on a shared curve they always sum to 2px, so the shadow's lower edge stays pinned exactly where it started and the button descends into it. One object, one motion.

Wrong · scale

scale() added. The shadow repaint and the GPU-composited scale run on different pipelines. A 1.5px hard-edged border shrinking against a repainting shadow desyncs — the border appears to glitch instead of reading as one press. This is the user-reported bug. Turn on slow motion and watch the border edge.

Wrong · shadow snap

Only transform transitioned. The shadow drops to zero on frame one, so the silhouette jumps 2px shorter and then the button drifts down to meet nothing. A flicker on every press. This was the open question and it is settled here: transition both.

/* CORRECT — ships in tokens.css as .stamp */
.pill{
  box-shadow: 0 2px 0 var(--ink);
  transition:
    transform  110ms var(--ease-out),
    box-shadow 110ms var(--ease-out);
}
.pill:active:not([disabled]){
  transform: translateY(2px);
  box-shadow: 0 0 0 var(--ink);
}
/* WRONG — both of these, for different reasons */
.pill:active{
  transform: translateY(2px) scale(.985);  /* desync */
  box-shadow: 0 0 0 var(--ink);
}

.pill{
  /* shadow snaps on frame 1 → flicker */
  transition: transform 110ms var(--ease-out);
}

Scope: this applies to every component carrying the stamp, not just the button. Today that is the primary pill and its sm and fab variants. If a future component takes the stamp, it takes this press with it.

CSS or GSAP?

CSS for state. Anything a pointer or a class toggle drives: presses, hovers, focus, drawer open/close, a chip filter turning on. CSS transitions are interruptible and retarget mid-flight; they also run off the main thread, so they stay smooth while TanStack Query is deserialising a year of transactions. Keyframes restart from zero — avoid them for anything a person can trigger twice quickly.

GSAP for sequence. The login constellation is the only place in Sorted that earns it: six chips staggered, a core scale, and a dashoffset draw that has to land in order. That is a timeline, and a timeline is what GSAP is for. Play it once, never on a loop — a repeating hero on a login screen reads as a spinner and implies the app is stuck. 

// same press, in GSAP — for when you need it
// on a timeline. Values are identical.
gsap.to(el, {
  y: 2, boxShadow: '0 0 0 #1A1A1A',
  duration: .11, ease: 'power4.out'
});           // never add scale

// the login hero. once:true, and the static
// SVG is already the final frame — animation
// is a layer, never a dependency.
gsap.matchMedia().add(
  '(prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)', () => {
  const tl = gsap.timeline();
  tl.from('#hero-core', {scale:.9, opacity:0,
      duration:.42, ease:'power3.out'})
    .from('#hero-chips .hc', {scale:.92, opacity:0,
      duration:.4, stagger:.06, ease:'power4.out'}, '-=.2')

Never animate a rupee figure. No count-ups, no odometer roll. The number is either known or it is a skeleton.

The ten rules
  1. Ask whether it should animate at all. Anything seen a hundred times a day gets no animation.
  2. Every animation answers “why”. If the answer is “it looks cool” and it's seen daily, cut it.
  3. Entering or exiting → --ease-out. Moving on screen → --ease-in-out. Never ease-in.
  4. UI motion stays under 300ms. Only the modal is allowed 400.
  5. Animate transform and opacity only. Never width, height, padding, margin.
  6. Never enter from scale(0). Start at 0.95–0.97 with opacity.
  7. Popovers scale from their trigger. Modals stay centred.
  8. Transitions, not keyframes, for anything a person can trigger twice quickly.
  9. Exit faster than enter. Slow where the user decides, fast where the system responds.
  10. Reduced motion means fewer and gentler, not zero: keep opacity and colour, drop movement.
Sorted's own additions
  1. The stamp press never scales. translateY matched to the shadow offset, both properties on one curve. Locked, from a real bug.
  2. No rupee figure ever animates. No count-up, no odometer. Known, or skeleton.
  3. No springs, no bounce, no overshoot anywhere in the product.
  4. The hero plays once. A looping hero on a login screen reads as a spinner.
  5. The static SVG is the final frame. Animation is a layer on top of a complete asset, never a dependency. If GSAP fails to load, the screen is still correct.
!

All motion in this product is reviewed by emil-design-eng.
Change an easing or a duration without consulting it and you're breaking the system.

§ 09

Accessibility

One person uses this every day for years. That is a stronger accessibility argument than compliance, not a weaker one — an interface you can't read at 6am in November is a broken tool.

RequirementStatus
1.4.3 Contrast (AA)Pass, with two documented restrictions — --dim is confined to mono micro-labels and --rule is a non-text exemption. Every ratio in §02 is computed.
1.4.1 Use of colourPass. Every chip carries a distinct glyph; colour is never the sole carrier of meaning. Direction on upcoming rows is an arrow, not a hue.
1.4.11 Non-text contrastPass. All six chip glyphs clear 3:1 against their fill (lowest 3.32:1). Every component boundary is ink at 16.08:1.
2.4.7 Focus visiblePass. 2.5px --focus, offset 3px outward. Never removed, never inset on a filled control.
2.3.3 Animation from interactionsPass. prefers-reduced-motion collapses every duration to 1ms, stops the skeleton pulse, and drops the press translate while keeping the shadow collapse so the state change stays legible.
1.4.4 Resize textPass to 200%. All type is px in a fixed single-user desktop layout; the browser's own zoom is the resize path and every panel reflows.
1.4.12 Text spacingPass. No fixed-height text containers; rows grow.
Hard rules for implementation
  • Never outline:none without an equivalent replacement in the same rule.
  • Focus rings are always offset outward. --focus on --ink is 1.37:1; an inset ring on the primary CTA is invisible.
  • Fields never go below 16px — iOS zooms the viewport on focus at 15px and under.
  • The tether is alt="". Its meaning is duplicated in the row's sub-label.
  • Every chip in the DOM needs an accessible name, either a <title> in the SVG or an aria-label on its container. A bare coloured circle says nothing.
  • Errors are announced. Notices carry role="alert"; the field carries aria-invalid and aria-describedby pointing at the notice.
  • Hover effects are gated behind @media (hover:hover) and (pointer:fine) so a tap doesn't leave a stuck hover state.
  • OTP boxes are one input, not six. Six inputs break paste, autofill, and every screen reader.
Edge cases the system already answers

Long merchant names. The row's middle column is 1fr and wraps to two lines; the amount column is auto and never compresses. A number is never truncated — a truncated rupee figure is a wrong rupee figure.

Large amounts. ₹1,86,42,000 at 108px overflows a narrow panel. Guilt-Free steps down to 78px above 8 glyphs; it does not shrink to fit.

Negative guilt-free money. The figure stays in ink, not red. --alert is for things that failed, not things that are true. The sub-label carries the news: “₹4,200 over. Two days left.”

Devanagari and long English. Geist and Geist Mono cover Latin; a Devanagari merchant name falls back to the system stack and grows the line height. The row grid handles it because nothing has a fixed height.

§ 11

Voice & tone

Sorted talks to exactly one person, who owns it, and who already knows what a SIP is. Plain, specific, unhurried, never congratulatory. It does not have a personality; it has manners.
